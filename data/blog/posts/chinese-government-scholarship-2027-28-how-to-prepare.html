If you want to study in China on a Chinese Government Scholarship (CSC) in the 2027/28 academic year, the best time to start is before the application opens, not on the day it does. Last year's notices appeared between late October and December, and some deadlines fell as early as 10 January. This guide explains what is known today, what changed for undergraduates, and a simple plan to get ready.

Last checked: 25 October 2026. Everything below comes from Chinese embassy and consulate notices and from the official CSCA exam website. The scholarship portal (campuschina.org) could not be opened by our tools when we checked, so always confirm the final details there and with your own embassy.

Where things stand today

We could not find a worldwide 2027/28 timetable yet. The only official 2027/28 notice we found is from the Chinese Embassy in South Sudan, posted on 23 October 2026. It applies to South Sudanese applicants only: apply online at campuschina.org, then send a photocopied hard-copy package to the embassy by 20 January 2027.

That is useful as a signal: notices are starting to appear. But deadlines, agency numbers and whether you need paper documents are set country by country. Your own embassy or consulate decides yours, so check its website every week from now until your notice appears.

The new CSCA exam for bachelor's applicants

If you are applying for a bachelor's degree, the biggest change is the China Scholastic Competency Assessment (CSCA). An embassy notice says bachelor's applicants from the 2026/27 academic year onward must take it before submitting their scholarship application. The 2027/28 South Sudan notice repeats this: you must take the exam by the application deadline and include the score report. The same embassy notice says that from 2028 all undergraduate applicants to Chinese universities will have to take it.

  • Subjects: Professional Chinese, Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry, each scored 0 to 100. Your target university decides which subjects your major needs, so ask before you register.
  • Where to register: csca.cn. You need a compliant photo, a valid government ID and a credit card. The fee is CNY 450 for one subject and CNY 700 for two or more.
  • Official exam dates (viewed 25 October 2026): 14 to 15 November 2026 (registration has closed), then 19 to 20 December 2026, 23 to 24 January, 13 to 14 March, 24 to 25 April and 26 to 27 June 2027. Registration for the sessions after November had not opened yet.
  • Scores: from the November 2026 session, scores are published within 10 working days. We found no national minimum score and no stated validity period; embassies say each university sets its own criteria.

Practical advice: if you apply for 2027/28 and your embassy deadline is in January or February, aim for the December or January session so your score report arrives in time.

Choose your route: Type A or Type B

You apply online through the Study in China site, campuschina.org, in both cases. The difference is who recommends you:

  • Type A (embassy route): you apply through your own country's embassy or receiving agency, using that agency's number. The embassy sends its recommended applicants to the China Scholarship Council, which makes the final decision.
  • Type B (university route): you apply through a Chinese university that runs a scholarship programme, and the university sets its own deadline.

One rule applies to all: the scholarship council grants no more than one scholarship per person, and holding two Chinese government scholarships at the same time means cancellation and repayment.

What the scholarship covers

The 2026/27 Sweden embassy notice lists:

  • tuition fees;
  • a free university dormitory or an accommodation subsidy (we found no official amount);
  • a monthly stipend of CNY 2,500 for undergraduates, CNY 3,000 for master's students and CNY 3,500 for doctoral students;
  • comprehensive medical insurance of CNY 800 per year.

International flights: embassies disagree. Two 2026/27 notices say international travel is not covered; one embassy page lists a return airfare. Do not budget on the assumption that your flight is paid, and read your own embassy's notice.

Your document checklist

  • the online application form;
  • a passport scan, valid for at least 12 months from your expected start date;
  • your highest diploma, notarised (or proof of student status if you have not graduated yet), and transcripts;
  • a pre-admission notice or invitation letter from at least one university you applied to. This is mandatory;
  • language proof: HSK 3 for Chinese-taught bachelor's programmes, HSK 4 for Chinese-taught master's and doctoral programmes, or IELTS/TOEFL or similar for programmes taught in another language, as the university requires;
  • a study plan of at least 200 words (bachelor's), 500 (non-degree) or 800 (postgraduate);
  • for master's and doctoral applicants, two recommendation letters from professors or associate professors;
  • the English Foreigner Physical Examination Form, completed by a doctor with a signature, a hospital stamp and a sealed photo (without all three it is invalid). Its results are valid for only 6 months, so do not take the exam too early;
  • a non-criminal-record certificate, usually issued within the last 6 months;
  • your CSCA score report if you apply for a bachelor's degree.

Age limits in the 2026/27 notices: under 25 for bachelor's, under 35 for master's and under 40 for doctoral applicants.

Mistakes the official notices warn about

  • Wrong agency number or category. The application does not reach the people who process it. Each embassy has its own number, so copy it from your own embassy's notice.
  • Incomplete or unreadable documents. Incomplete applications are rejected automatically, and you carry the risk of blurry scans.
  • Editing after submitting. You can withdraw and edit an application only before processing starts, and you must submit it again afterwards, or it will not be processed.
  • Hiding other funding. One embassy notice says deliberately hiding funding leads to disqualification and a three-year ban.
  • Paying someone for a place. The Chinese Embassy in Ireland says the scholarship council has not authorised any individual or agency to handle applications. Nobody can sell you a place. Anyone who helps you, including us, can only help you prepare; the decision is made by the council.
  • Different paper rules. Some embassies wanted no paper at all last year, while the South Sudan 2027/28 notice wants a photocopy package. Follow your own embassy.

A simple plan from now until the application opens

  1. This week: find your embassy's scholarship page and bookmark it. Decide Type A or Type B.
  2. By mid-November: shortlist two or three universities and programmes, and ask each for a pre-admission notice.
  3. Bachelor's applicants: choose your CSCA subjects with your target university and register for the December or January session.
  4. November to December: order your notarised diploma and transcripts, ask two professors for recommendation letters, and write your study plan.
  5. When your embassy notice appears: note the deadline and agency number, book the physical examination and the police certificate so that they are still valid, then apply online well before the deadline.
