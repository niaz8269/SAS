If you are planning to start your studies in 2027, the rules in the four biggest English-speaking destinations are moving. Some changes are already law, some have a start date, and some are only proposals or are held up in court. This guide separates the three, country by country, using official government pages, and then shows what the changes mean for your choice.

Last checked: 25 October 2026. Immigration rules change often. Treat this as a map of what to ask, and confirm every figure on the official page linked beside it before you pay for anything.

United Kingdom

  • Graduate visa shorter from 2027. The official Graduate visa page says it lasts 2 years if you apply on or before 31 December 2026 and 18 months if you apply on or after 1 January 2027. What counts is the date of your application, not the date you receive your degree. PhD graduates still get 3 years. The change is set out in the Home Office's explanatory memorandum to HC 1333 (14 October 2025).
  • Work limits. Home Office caseworker guidance (updated 16 October 2026) says degree-level students at a compliant university may work up to 20 hours a week in term time and full time in vacations; students below degree level may work 10 hours.
  • Money. The Student visa fee is £558. The money page currently shows £1,529 a month in London or £1,171 a month elsewhere for up to 9 months, plus your first-year course fee, held for 28 days. The Home Office says the requirement rises from 30 November 2026 to match home-student maintenance loans (HC 584), but we did not find the new amounts, so check that page before you prepare your bank statements.
  • Dependants. Students can bring family only if they are government-sponsored on a course longer than 6 months or on a PhD or research-based higher degree (courses starting on or after 1 January 2024).
  • Only a draft: a levy of £925 per international student, charged to universities (not to you), is planned for 1 August 2028 and is still draft legislation.

Canada

  • Work. Up to 24 hours a week off campus during the academic session, and unlimited hours in scheduled breaks.
  • The cap. IRCC expects to issue up to 408,000 study permits in 2026. Since 1 January 2026, master's and doctoral students at public designated institutions no longer need a provincial attestation letter, and 49,000 permits are set aside for them. We found no 2027 cap yet.
  • After graduation. The PGWP needs language results in all four abilities: CLB 7 for university programmes and CLB 5 for college programmes. Bachelor's, master's and doctoral graduates need no field-of-study match. A permit can last up to 3 years.
  • Money. From 1 September 2026 you must show CAD 23,448 for living costs (outside Quebec), on top of tuition and travel.
  • Spouses. An open work permit for a spouse is limited to students in a master's programme of 16 months or longer, a doctorate or certain professional programmes.

Australia

For Australia we could read only the government's own study portal, because the immigration department's pages did not open for us. Confirm key points on the Department of Home Affairs site.

  • Work. 48 hours a fortnight in term; no limit in breaks, and no limit for research master's and PhD students.
  • Higher charges. From 1 July 2026 the Student visa charge rose from AUD 2,000 to AUD 2,500 and the Temporary Graduate (485) charge from AUD 4,600 to AUD 5,750.
  • Onshore applications. From 2 October 2026, most temporary visa holders cannot apply for a Student visa while in Australia, and family members generally cannot be included in student visa applications unless an exemption applies. Apply from outside Australia and plan for it.
  • Numbers. The government set the 2027 National Planning Level at 295,000 new international students, the same as 2026. It describes this as a way to prioritise visa processing rather than a cap.
  • After graduation. The 485 visa usually runs 2 to 3 years for the higher-education stream and generally requires you to be 35 or under.

United States

  • Work and OPT. F-1 students may work up to 20 hours a week on campus. After study, OPT is generally 12 months, and graduates with a STEM degree on the official list can apply for a 24-month extension.
  • Fixed stay: postponed. The Department of Homeland Security published a final rule on 17 July 2026 that would replace "duration of status" with a fixed admission period of up to four years and a 30-day grace period. It was due to start on 15 September 2026, but on 14 September 2026 a federal court postponed it, and USCIS says it is using the previous rules. The case is still going on and the order can change, so check USCIS and Study in the States before you plan your stay. (Some official pages still describe the rule without mentioning the court order.)
  • OPT fee: only a proposal. On 7 October 2026 DHS proposed that schools pay $70,000 the first time they recommend a student for OPT and $30,000 for each later recommendation. Public comments close on 9 November 2026. Nothing has changed yet, and the fee would fall on schools, although schools might pass it on.
  • Funds. There is no single dollar figure: each school states your required funds on your I-20.

What this means for you

  1. Plan the whole route, not just the visa. Look at working hours, how long you may stay after graduating, and what happens to your family before you pay a deposit.
  2. Apply early where a change has a date. A UK application after 31 December 2026 gets the shorter Graduate visa. Plan the date of your application, not just your start date.
  3. Do not rely on a proposal or on rumours. The US OPT fee and the UK levy are not law. Equally, a rule that a court postponed can return.
  4. Ask for the rule in writing. If an agent says "the visa is guaranteed", ask for the official page. No one can guarantee a visa.
  5. Compare more than four countries. Several other destinations publish clear rules for graduates. For example, DAAD says non-EU graduates of German universities can get up to 18 months to look for qualified work and may take any job meanwhile. Campus France lists a 12-month job-seeker permit for master's-level graduates (the page is undated, so confirm with the prefecture).
